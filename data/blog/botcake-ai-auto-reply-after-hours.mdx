# After-hours auto-reply with Botcake AI — set it up so the AI doesn't trample your team

> **TL;DR:** A customer messages at 10pm, your team left hours ago — **silence costs you the sale, but an AI running unchecked costs you control the next morning**. Three options here, simple to advanced: (A) **Meta Away Message** — no Botcake needed, 5 minutes; (B) **Botcake AI on night duty** — real conversations, real lead capture; (C) **Botcake + POS/CRM + outbound alerts** — the AI files the lead and pings your team. The part that matters most: **turning on "Pause Botcake AI when staff replies"** so the AI stops talking over your people.

---

## The problem: customers don't wait for business hours

> *"Our page doesn't operate after 5pm and we're back at 8am. Is there a way to auto-reply to customers between 5pm and 8am?"*

This question comes up almost weekly in support. Not because the feature is missing — but because there are **several ways to do it**, and each suits a different size of shop.

Two things go wrong when it's set up carelessly:

1. **AI runs 24/7** → next morning your agent replies, the AI chimes in too → the customer gets two overlapping messages and the thread turns into a mess
2. **Only a default message is enabled** → it promises "we'll get back to you" but never asks for a name or phone number → in the morning nobody knows who to call first

Each option below is ordered by effort. Pick the one that fits your operation.

---

## Choosing an option

| | **A — Away Message** | **B — Botcake AI on schedule** | **C — Botcake + POS/CRM** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Botcake required?** | No | Yes | Yes |
| **Setup time** | 5 min | 30 min | 2–4 hours |
| **Can it actually advise?** | No | Yes | Yes (advanced) |
| **Captures name/phone?** | Not automatically | Yes | Yes |
| **Creates orders / leads?** | No | No | Yes (POS / CRM) |
| **Best for** | New shops, no Botcake yet | Most shops | Shops needing triage and handoff |

## Option A — Meta Business Suite Away Message (no Botcake)

A free feature of **Meta Business Suite**, unrelated to Pancake. Use it when you only need to **tell customers your hours** and don't need the AI to advise anyone yet.

### Setup

1. Go to **Meta Business Suite (business.facebook.com) → Inbox → Automated responses**
2. Pick **Away message → Edit**
3. Turn on **Status** and choose **Scheduled**
4. Enter your hours (e.g. 8:00–17:00) — the message fires outside that window
5. Write the message — template below

**After-hours message template:**

```
Thanks for messaging [Shop name]! 🙏

We're closed right now — our hours are 8:00–17:00, Monday to Saturday.

Leave your phone number and we'll call you first thing when we're back!
```

> [!TIP]
> Keep the *"leave your phone number"* line. Next morning your team filters unread conversations in Pancake and immediately sees who left a number worth calling first.

> [!NOTE]
> Meta's Away Message **asks nothing back** and can't discuss products — it fires one message and goes quiet. If you want something that actually asks for a name, a number, and answers product questions, you need Option B.

## Option B — Botcake AI on night duty

The most common setup. Botcake AI covers the hours your team doesn't.

### Step 1 — Create an AI Agent

Log into **Botcake**, left sidebar under **ADVANCE → Botcake AI**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Agents belong to a page.** Pick the right page in the top-left **before** creating an Agent — there's no step later to assign a page to an Agent. If you run several pages, each one needs its own Agent, even with identical content.

![Botcake AI screen: ADVANCE menu on the left, agent list and the Create new Agent button](/static/images/blog/botcake/botcake-ai-danh-sach-agent.png)

> [!NOTE]
> The screenshot above shows the Vietnamese interface. Botcake ships both languages — *Danh sách Agent* is **Agent list**, *Tạo Agent mới* is **Create new Agent**.

The **CATEGORY** column has five entries; you'll use the first three:

| Entry | What it's for |
|---|---|
| **Agent list** | Create and manage Agents. One Agent can serve several features |
| **Information** | General shop details — shared by every Agent |
| **Knowledge source** | Where price lists, product descriptions and policies live |
| **Statistics** | How many conversations the AI handled and what it consumed |
| **General settings** | Configuration applied to every Agent on the page |

Hit **+ Create new Agent** (top right) and give it a recognisable name — *Night Shift AI*, for instance.

> [!NOTE]
> Your first Agent lands under **DEFAULT AGENT** — the one used when nothing else is specified. Later Agents sit under **ALL AGENTS** and need to be attached to a specific feature before they do anything.

### Step 2 — Write the AI's instructions

Open the Agent you just made. The detail screen has **four tabs**:

| Tab | Contents |
|---|---|
| **Instruction** | The AI's instructions — the most important part, written in this step |
| **Tools** | Enable Pancake POS and CRM so the AI can create orders and leads |
| **Knowledge** | Pick which documents this Agent may use |
| **AI response behavior** | Fine-tune how the AI phrases replies |

Top right holds the **model dropdown** (GPT-4.1 mini, …). A stronger model answers better but burns credit faster — for after-hours work a light model is usually plenty.

> [!TIP]
> The right-hand panel has a **Preview** pane with **Click here to test your AI** — chat with it inside Botcake without touching the live page. Use it to iterate on the prompt before going live.

Under the **Instruction** tab, the **Create your instructions** box is where the prompt goes.

#### The three-part structure Botcake uses

Botcake's own sample prompt follows a fixed skeleton, and the AI obeys it far more reliably than free-form writing:

```
1: Context
   - You are [AI name], a customer care agent for [Shop name]
   - Write warmly and conversationally, use light emoji to sound natural
   - The shop is currently closed. Hours: 8:00–17:00, Monday to Saturday

2: Goal
   - Give enough advice that the customer doesn't have to wait until morning
   - Collect the following so a human can call back tomorrow:
     + Name
     + Phone number
     + Which product or service they're interested in

3. Process
   Note: follow every step below, in order, without exception
   - Step 1: Greet and state that we're closed
     + Greet the customer
     + Say the shop is outside working hours and a human will follow up at 8:00
     + (Do not ask "how can I help?" — proactively ask which product they're after)
   - Step 2: Advise using Knowledge
     + You must pull product details from Knowledge. Never invent them
     + If Knowledge has no answer, say "I'll have a colleague confirm that for you in the morning"
   - Step 3: Capture details
     + Ask for name and phone number so someone can call back
     + Read the details back to confirm
     + Thank them and restate when a human will reach out
```

Why this skeleton works:

| Part | What it buys you |
|---|---|
| **Context** | Locks tone and persona — no drifting between formal and casual mid-thread |
| **Goal** | Names **exactly** what to collect, so the AI knows when it's done |
| **Process** | Forces order. *"Follow every step below, in order, without exception"* is the line doing the heavy lifting |

> [!TIP]
> Three techniques worth stealing from Botcake's sample prompt:
>
> 1. **Negative instructions in parentheses** — *"(Do not ask 'how can I help?' — proactively ask…)"*. Models love opening with an empty question; stating what **not** to do works better than describing what to do.
> 2. **Spell out what "collected" means** — the original sample writes *"Delivery address (must include house number, street, ward, district, city)"*. Leave it vague and the AI will accept "Hanoi" and call it a day.
> 3. **Make data lookups mandatory** — *"You must call the product system to retrieve…"*. Without the word *must*, the AI guesses instead of looking things up.

> [!NOTE]
> The **Improve AI** button (top right) asks the AI to rewrite your prompt more clearly. Draft it roughly in the three-part skeleton, then hit the button — faster than polishing wording yourself. Re-read the result, though; it sometimes drops a constraint you added on purpose.

> [!TIP]
> The **Business information** card above the prompt box holds shop name, address and hours centrally. Fill it in once and your prompts can just say *"per the business information"* instead of repeating it in every Agent.

### Step 3 — Put product data in Knowledge, not the prompt

This is where shops commonly go wrong: dumping the entire price list into the prompt. Long prompts respond slower, cost more per message, and every price change means editing every Agent.

Knowledge works at **two levels**:

| Level | Path | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Shared library** | Botcake AI → **Knowledge source** | The page's document store. Add, edit and delete here |
| **Per Agent** | Agent → **Knowledge** tab | **Select** which documents this Agent may use |

Documents go into the shared library first, then get selected per Agent. One price list can serve several Agents.

Worth uploading:

- Product / plan price lists
- Product descriptions and specs
- Returns, warranty and shipping policies
- Frequently asked questions

Open the Agent → **Knowledge** tab → **Add files**:

![Agent Knowledge tab with two Excel files selected: a pricing sheet and a sales list](/static/images/blog/botcake/botcake-agent-knowledge.png)

Each file shows its size and upload date. Hover for **preview** and **delete** icons.

**The payoff:** change a price in one library file and every Agent using it updates — no reopening Agents to edit prompts.

> [!TIP]
> **Files and URLs behave differently:**
>
> - **Uploaded files** (Excel, PDF…) are stored in the knowledge base — the AI reads the stored copy. Changing the content means uploading a new file
> - **URL sources** are fetched **live from the website on every answer** (using the Botcake AI GPT-5-nano model), so the information is always current
>
> If your pricing changes often, point a **URL at your live pricing page** instead of uploading a spreadsheet — one less thing to remember to update.

> [!TIP]
> The **Information** entry (in the CATEGORY column) is for fixed shop details: name, address, hours, hotline. Fill it once and skip repeating it in every Agent's prompt.

### Step 4 — Let the AI run outside working hours

This is **the most counter-intuitive part of the whole setup**, so read carefully.

Botcake has **no separate schedule for the AI**. It has **one set of working hours** for the whole page, and you then choose **which features are allowed to keep running once those hours end**.

Go to **Settings → Operating hours**:

![Botcake Operating hours screen: Working hours and the four Actions outside working hours toggles](/static/images/blog/botcake/botcake-operating-hours.png)

Two sections:

**1. Working hours — declare when your team is ACTUALLY around**

- **Operating time**: the window only applies when this is on
- **Time setting**: defaults to **08:00 – 23:00 daily**. Choose **Custom** to match your real hours (e.g. 08:00 – 17:00)

> [!WARNING]
> That 08:00–23:00 default fools a lot of shops into thinking they're done. If you close at 5pm but leave the default, the system treats 5pm–11pm as **inside working hours** — the AI stays quiet and customers get nothing. Click **Custom**.

**2. Actions outside working hours — what keeps running after close**

Four toggles. Whatever you switch on **continues operating outside hours**:

| Toggle | Switching it on means |
|---|---|
| **Skip Welcome messages** | Welcome messages still send after hours |
| **Skip Default messages** | Default messages still send after hours |
| **Skip Keywords** | Keyword flows still run after hours |
| **Skip BotcakeAI** | **The AI still advises after hours** ← the one you want |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **"Skip"** here means *skip the working-hours restriction*, **not** *disable the feature*. Turning on `Skip BotcakeAI` **exempts** the AI from the schedule, so it runs after hours. Plenty of people read it backwards, switch it off, and then wonder why the AI went silent.

### Two ways to get the AI running after hours

| Approach | What you do | When to use it |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Drop the schedule** | Turn **Operating time** off | You want the AI and all automation running 24/7, no distinction |
| **B. Exempt just the AI** | **Operating time** on + **Skip BotcakeAI** on | Keep the schedule for everything else; only the AI works nights |

**Approach B is the right one for after-hours coverage** — the schedule still governs Welcome/Default/Keywords while the AI takes the night shift.

> [!TIP]
> If you want customers to hear **only** from the AI after hours, leave `Skip Welcome messages` and `Skip Default messages` off. Turn on all three and someone messaging at 10pm can receive a welcome message, then a default message, then the AI's reply — three messages for one question.

> [!NOTE]
> This schedule belongs to **Botcake** and is independent of Meta Business Suite's Away Message. Run both and customers get two messages from two systems. Pick one.

### When the AI and a default message both qualify

The switch at the top right of the **Botcake AI** screen — **Prioritize default message ↔ Prioritize AI** — decides which one fires when both match:

| Switch position | Result |
|---|---|
| **Prioritize default message** | Default messages go first; the AI only runs when none match |
| **Prioritize AI** | The AI handles it; default messages are the fallback when the AI isn't active |

Choose **Prioritize AI** — you want a conversation that captures a phone number, not a canned line.

Two things that are easy to confuse: `Skip BotcakeAI` answers *"may the AI run outside hours?"*, while the priority switch answers *"when both the AI and a default message could fire, which wins?"*.

### Step 5 — Pause the AI when staff reply (THE IMPORTANT ONE)

This is the **most commonly skipped step**, and it causes the most common failure: next morning an agent answers the customer, and the AI keeps talking anyway.

It's a **page-level setting**, not a per-Agent one.

Go to **Settings → Pancake conversation** and scroll to **When staff replies on Pancake**:

| Group | Toggle | Recommended |
|---|---|---|
| **WITH BOTCAKE** | `Pause Bot` | On, if you also run keyword/flow bots |
| **WITH BOTCAKE AI** | `Pause Botcake AI` | **On** |
| | `Pause duration` | Defaults to **24 hours** |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Bot and Botcake AI are two separate toggles.** If you run both a flow bot and the AI but only switch on one, the other still interrupts your staff.

**How long should the pause be?**

| Duration | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **24 hours** (default) | An agent replies in the morning and owns that thread all day. Safest, but the AI won't help on that thread tonight either |
| **8–12 hours** | Covers one shift, then the AI picks the thread back up overnight. Suits shops wanting continuous night coverage |

If your main goal is *night coverage*, pick **8–12 hours**. If it's *never letting the AI step on a human*, keep **24 hours**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Without this toggle:
> - Your agent messages the customer at 8:30am
> - The AI is still active on that thread
> - The AI sends one more message → the customer hears two voices → trust gone

### Pausing the AI on a single conversation

Beyond the automatic rule above, you can **pause by hand** per conversation. In the Pancake chat view, click the Botcake icon in the conversation toolbar:

![Botcake popover inside a Pancake conversation showing Bot and AI rows, each with a pause button](/static/images/blog/botcake/botcake-pause-ai-conversation.png)

The popover shows **Bot** and **AI** separately, each with its own pause button. Use it when:

- A customer is upset and needs a human immediately
- The AI is answering wrongly and you want to cut it off mid-thread
- A VIP conversation you want kept entirely human

The arrow icon beside **Botcake** jumps straight into Botcake if you need to adjust something deeper.

**Testing before you go live — three stages:**

**Stage 1 — Test the content inside Botcake.** Open the Agent and use the **Preview** pane on the right, then **Click here to test your AI**. Run through the questions customers actually ask and watch whether the AI sticks to Knowledge and asks for a phone number. This stage can't verify the schedule or the pause rule.

![Preview pane on the right of the Agent screen with the Click here to test your AI button](/static/images/blog/botcake/botcake-agent-preview.png)

**Stage 2 — Test on the live page, visible only to you.** Before opening it to customers, go to **Settings → General → Customize Bot → Receive replies** and select **`AI replies to admins only`**.

![Botcake General settings: Timezone set to GMT+7 and the Receive replies options](/static/images/blog/botcake/botcake-general-settings.png)

In this mode the AI **only answers page admins** — real customers messaging in get nothing from it. Message the page from an admin account after hours to verify the schedule and the content without exposing anyone to a half-finished prompt.

The three options here:

| Option | Who gets answered |
|---|---|
| `Bot replies to everyone` | Everyone — live mode |
| `Bot replies to admins only` | Admins only, applied to flow bots |
| `AI replies to admins only` | Admins only, applied to the AI — **use while testing** |

**Stage 3 — Open it up.** Switch back to `Bot replies to everyone`. Message the page from a non-admin account after hours → confirm the AI replies → next morning have an agent reply in that thread → the AI must **not** add anything. Only this stage proves `Skip BotcakeAI` and `Pause Botcake AI` are actually working.

## Option C — Botcake with POS/CRM plus outbound alerts

Botcake integrates **directly** with Pancake POS and CRM — no Make.com needed for that part. Make.com only enters the picture when you want to reach services **outside the Pancake ecosystem**: Slack, email, Google Sheets.

### Enabling POS / CRM in the Agent

Open the Agent → **Tools** tab. The **BUILT-IN** section offers two integrations; enable whichever matches what you want the AI to do:

![Agent Tools tab showing the two built-in integrations, Pancake POS and Pancake CRM](/static/images/blog/botcake/botcake-agent-tools.png)

| Integration | What the AI can do | Fits |
|---|---|---|
| **Pancake POS** — *Automation create order* | Create orders automatically | Customer has decided; you just need the order placed |
| **Pancake CRM** — *CRM Lead Automation* | Create leads automatically | Customer is still exploring; a human should call back |

**For after-hours work, Pancake CRM is usually the better fit.** Someone messaging at 10pm is typically still researching rather than buying — filing a lead for a morning callback beats having the AI place an order overnight. Only shops with simple products and unambiguous pricing should enable POS as well.

Once enabled, **add a constraint to the prompt** in the *Process* section — otherwise the AI will file records while details are still missing:

```
- Step 4: Only create an order/lead once you have ALL of the following
   + Name
   + Phone number
   + Product of interest
   + Delivery address (if creating an order)
  Before creating anything, read the details back for confirmation.
  If the customer hasn't confirmed, do not create it.
```

> [!TIP]
> The **TOOLS** section below (the **Add new** button) is for custom tools — calling your own API, checking stock in an external system, and so on. That needs a developer and isn't required for after-hours coverage.

### The full flow with Make.com

Bring in Make.com when you need to **notify staff through an outside channel** (Slack, email, SMS):

```
Customer messages after hours
    → Botcake AI advises and captures details
    → [Direct] Creates an order in Pancake POS or a lead in CRM
    → Pancake webhook pushes the conversation event out
    → Make.com notifies the responsible agent via Slack / email
```

### Use Pancake's webhook, not a Botcake one

The outbound part runs on **Pancake's webhook** — configured in Pancake, not inside Botcake. Botcake AI handles the conversation; Pancake handles emitting the event.

The short version (full details in the [Pancake Webhook & API guide](/en/blog/pancake-webhook-and-api-send-message#2-configure-webhook--receive-real-time-events-from-pancake)):

1. **Ask Pancake Support to enable webhooks** — it can't be switched on from the interface, and only admin users can use it
2. In Pancake's **Webhook/API** section, paste the **Webhook URL** Make.com gives you
3. Your receiving endpoint must use **HTTPS** and return **200 within a few seconds** — if processing takes longer, acknowledge with 200 immediately and do the heavy work in the background

The payload Pancake sends already carries what a notification needs:

| Field | Use it for |
|---|---|
| `page_id` | Knowing which page the message came from, if you run several |
| `conversation_id` | Building a link that opens the thread directly |
| `customer` | Customer name and ID |
| `message` | What the customer actually wrote |

> [!TIP]
> No server yet? Grab a temporary URL from [webhook.site](https://webhook.site), paste it into Pancake, send a test message to the page, and inspect the real payload before building anything in Make.com.

### The notification scenario in Make.com

```
[Webhook] Receive event from Pancake
    → [Filter] Only messages outside working hours
    → [Slack / Email] Notify the responsible agent
        with customer name, message, and a link to the thread
```

> [!NOTE]
> The customer's phone number is **collected by the AI during the conversation** — it isn't in the webhook payload. To get it into the notification, have the AI write it to CRM/POS via the direct integration above and let Make.com read it back — or accept a notification that just links to the thread.

> [!NOTE]
> Option C needs someone comfortable with Make.com, or a developer on hand. If all you need is the AI creating orders and CRM records, the direct integrations are enough — skip Make.com entirely.

## Common problems and fixes

### The AI doesn't reply even though it's enabled

| Cause | Check |
|---|---|
| The conversation is assigned to a specific agent | Remove the assignment and the AI resumes |
| Still inside the pause window after a staff reply | Open the thread → click the Botcake icon → re-enable **AI** |
| `Skip BotcakeAI` never switched on | **Settings → Operating hours**, enable `Skip BotcakeAI` |
| Working hours left at the 08:00–23:00 default | Click **Custom** and set your real closing time |
| Schedule off because of timezone | **Settings → General → Timezone** must be `(GMT +7:00) Bangkok, Hanoi, Jakarta` |
| Agent created on the wrong page | Agents belong to the selected page. Check the page in the top-left, then look under **Botcake AI → Agent list** |
| Left on `AI replies to admins only` | Switch back to `Bot replies to everyone` under **Settings → General** |

### The AI doesn't stop when staff reply

| Cause | Fix |
|---|---|
| `Pause Botcake AI` not enabled | Enable it under **Settings → Pancake conversation → When staff replies on Pancake** |
| Only `Pause Bot` enabled, `Pause Botcake AI` forgotten | They're separate toggles — enable both if you run both |
| Agent replied from mobile and Pancake hasn't registered it | Wait for sync (usually under 30 seconds) or refresh |
| `Pause duration` set too short | Raise it to 8–24 hours to cover a full shift |

### The AI gets product details wrong

| Cause | Fix |
|---|---|
| Knowledge source missing the product data | Add the price list and policies under **Botcake AI → Knowledge source** |
| AI inventing product names from ad copy | Add a rule: *"Only discuss products present in Knowledge. Never name a product after an ad"* |
| New products never added | Update Knowledge every time you add a product |
| Prompt doesn't force the AI to stay grounded | Add: *"If Knowledge has no answer, say a colleague will confirm"* |

> [!TIP]
> After updating Knowledge, **start a fresh test conversation** rather than continuing the old one — the AI retains context within a thread and will keep answering from the stale version.

## FAQ

**Q: Which option doesn't require buying Botcake?**
A: **Option A** uses Meta Business Suite's Away Message — free, no Botcake, no extra Pancake plan. But it's one canned message with no conversation.

**Q: Can the AI run during business hours too?**
A: It can, though usually it shouldn't. During the day your team needs control of the thread, and parallel AI replies mean two voices answering one customer. A clear schedule works better.

**Q: The AI captured a phone number overnight — how does the team handle it in the morning?**
A: Filter conversations by **unread** in Pancake and prioritise threads with a number and clear intent. With Option C, staff get a notification before the shift even starts.

**Q: Can I kill the AI in an emergency?**
A: Two levels. **One conversation:** click the Botcake icon in that thread and pause **AI**. **Whole page:** go to **Settings → Operating hours** and turn off `Skip BotcakeAI`, or disable the Agent entirely under **Botcake AI → Agent list**.

**Q: Does Botcake AI cost extra?**
A: Botcake includes a **free tier of 500,000 messages** — check consumption in the bottom-left **USAGE → Free message**. Small and mid-sized shops running the AI overnight rarely hit that ceiling.

The model chosen in the Agent's top-right dropdown (GPT-4.1 mini, …) determines how fast credit burns. A light model handles after-hours work fine — greeting, quoting from a price list, capturing a number. Track usage under **Botcake AI → Statistics**.

## Wrapping up

There's no single correct configuration — picking the right option matters more than building the most elaborate one:

- **New shop, no Botcake yet:** Option A, done in 5 minutes
- **Moderate volume, want real conversations:** Option B — and the step that matters most is **pausing the AI when staff reply**
- **Need triage and post-conversation automation:** Option C with POS/CRM and outbound alerts

Whichever you choose, **the minimum bar** is that customers know you're closed and know how to leave their details. Don't let someone message at midnight and find silence in the morning — not because you ignored them, but because they never knew the message landed.
